# Research brief

Decision: identify defensible workflow and requirements hypotheses for a reusable
hospital management product serving small private Ethiopian hospitals and medical
centers, using public evidence because the founder has no current hospital contact.

Authorized method: native web research; no outreach and no private patient data.
Use published interviews/qualitative studies, authoritative operating standards,
implementation studies, and documented or transcribed workflow demonstrations.

Breadth-first dimensions:
1. Ethiopian practitioner/patient workflow evidence and workarounds.
2. Ethiopian implementation and operational lessons across departments.
3. Training, demonstrations, interviews and documentary evidence.
4. Official workflow guidance and requirement synthesis (parent).

Standard effort: three research assistants, up to eight read sources per
dimension per round, maximum two rounds, normal verification of load-bearing
claims. Stop early on coverage. Search surface: public web; no specialized
hospital knowledge-base connector is available. Expected duration: several minutes.

Separate observed practices, prescriptive standards, vendor demonstrations,
engineering recommendations, and unresolved customer choices. Public-hospital
or foreign evidence is not automatically representative of Ethiopian private
hospitals. Historical studies describe their study setting and date, not current
national prevalence. Do not claim to have watched a video without inspected
video content or a transcript; label description-only sources accordingly.

Deliverables: research.md, source digests, workflow map, proposed requirements
with acceptance examples and evidence links, and open validation questions.
No application implementation or claim of completed clinical validation.
