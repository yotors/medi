# Independent verification: implementation claims

Date/accessed: **2026-10-12**. Scope: read only `implementation-r1.md`; used three web calls to check original research. No facility percentages were reproduced or independently validated. Existing percentages remain findings of their individual studies.

## Verdict

**Partial departmental adoption is independently corroborated.** A different publisher and older underlying sample show that registration/database presence does not establish complete clinical use and that laboratory/pharmacy staff identified other departments’ nonuse as an obstacle.

**Downtime is independently corroborated; the exact paper-to-EMR re-entry practice is not independently replicated by the additional Ethiopian samples inspected.** Its direct support remains an interview account in implementation source I3. A requirement to define recovery and reconcile temporary records is a reasonable design inference, not a universally observed Ethiopian procedure or regulatory mandate.

## Independent source A: different publisher and sample

[Comprehensive Evaluation of Electronic Medical Record System Use and User Satisfaction at Five Low-Resource Setting Hospitals in Ethiopia](https://medinform.jmir.org/2015/2/e22/), Tilahun and Fritz. Publisher: **JMIR Publications**, *JMIR Medical Informatics*. Published **2015-05-25**; study **January–February 2014**, with questionnaire collection described as one month. Accessed **2026-10-12**. Class: original survey, server-record assessment, and power monitoring; five **government hospitals in Addis Ababa**, 406 respondents.

Supported observations: laboratory/pharmacy respondents identified other departments’ nonuse as an obstacle; server records distinguished registered patients from patients with a recorded main diagnosis. Staff were expected to maintain both paper and electronic records. Power monitoring documented interruption and inadequate generator coverage for full EMR operation.

Confidence: **moderate for historical mechanisms**, low for present prevalence or small-private-facility transfer. This is a separate sample and publisher from I1–I5. It does **not** establish a standardized post-outage re-entry procedure. Routine dual documentation must not be relabelled downtime reconciliation. Publication contains some numerical inconsistencies; no percentages are adopted here.

## Independent source B: recent separate underlying sample

[Evaluating the impact of electronic medical records on healthcare digitalization efforts in Oromia, Ethiopia: A qualitative study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20552076251379734), Tiya et al. Publisher: **SAGE**, *DIGITAL HEALTH*. Published **2025-09-15**; study **February 2023–November 2024**. Accessed **2026-10-12**. Class: interviews and observation; 29 participants associated with public Adama Hospital Medical College, Ambo University Referral Hospital, and supporting organizations.

Supported observations: a respondent described paperless central triage alongside incomplete laboratory/pharmacy digitization. Other respondents described internet/power interruptions, slow generator startup, and needing IT assistance from the main campus. These directly corroborate uneven departmental adoption and interruption risk in another setting, but do not document the specific paper-re-entry workflow.

Confidence: **moderate for reported experience**. This is a different sample from I2 and I3; publisher differs from I3 but is shared with I2. Purposive selection of partial implementations limits prevalence inference. Some departments were excluded from interviews, so this cannot verify comprehensive adoption status.

## Rechecked direct claim: not an independent replication

[Barriers to the implementation of electronic medical records in Northwest Ethiopia](https://scielo.org.za/pdf/hsa/v30/102.pdf), Tsehay and Matlhaba. Publisher: **AOSIS**, *Health SA Gesondheid*. Published **2025-09-20**; collection period not established; accessed **2026-10-12**. Class: qualitative interviews across five urban Bahir Dar facilities, mixed ownership.

The public PDF’s page 5 includes an account of retaining parallel paper records during slowdowns and re-entering information following power downtime. This verifies that I3’s wording has a direct interview basis, rather than only the authors’ recommendation. It is the **same underlying study**, including when hosted by PMC or SciELO, and therefore cannot be counted as independent corroboration. The account does not validate reconciliation accuracy, duplicate rates, responsible staff, or applicability to every private facility.

## Wording safe for synthesis

“Multiple Ethiopian studies, including public-hospital evidence from separate publishers and periods, identify incomplete departmental adoption and infrastructure interruption as implementation risks. One mixed-ownership qualitative study specifically describes paper fallback followed by electronic re-entry. Consequently, complete cross-department pilot journeys and explicit recovery/reconciliation procedures are proposed requirements for validation with target facilities.”

Do not upgrade this into “Ethiopian hospitals all re-enter paper records after outages,” “offline software is universally required,” or a quantified national adoption/outage estimate. Recovery design, reconciliation ownership, duplicate prevention and support service levels remain implementation inferences requiring local walkthroughs.
